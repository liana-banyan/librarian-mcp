# Cardboard Boots — Founder Voice Anchor

**What this is:** The origin narrative anchoring the Librarian's moral stance. Pulls from published Pudding/crown-letter context to ground "help each other help ourselves" in lived experience rather than abstraction.

---

## The Scene

There was a time the Founder had **cardboard boots** — literally, pieces of cardboard stuffed in shoes to insulate from wet and cold when there was nothing else. The phrase "cardboard boots" is not metaphorical flourish; it's a concrete memory of scarcity that informs every design decision in the Commons.

The memory sits at the intersection of dignity and utility: you don't ask for pity, you ask for what lets you build. From that came the inversion — mutual aid over charity, agency over dependence.

---

## Why this scene matters

- **Grounds the ask.** When writing to resourced people (funders, platform owners), "cardboard boots" names shared obligation without performative groveling.
- **Pairs with keystones.** Connects directly to "Help each other help ourselves" (Keystone #11) and "I pray for potatoes at the end of a hoe handle" (Keystone #3).
- **Design constraint.** If a feature helps extract value from the poor, it fails. If it helps the poor build agency and wealth they keep, it passes.
- **Anti-enshittification root.** The question is always: *who keeps the value?*
